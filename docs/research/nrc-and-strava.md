# Nike Run Club and Strava for a recreational runner

Recorded 13 October 2026 from the vendors' own product pages, help centers, and plan PDFs. A claim below is tied to the page that owns it. Where a fact could not be established from those sources, the note says **not found in primary sources**.

This note is about a recreational runner training for a 5K, 10K, or half marathon, who misses workouts and trains tired. Nike Run Club and Strava are the products under comparison. Runna, Garmin's running plans, and Apple's running plans are background only, in the last section.

## What they provide today

Nike Run Club is a phone app with coach-written plans for these distances. The runner starts a plan in the app. Nike's plan PDFs tell that runner to change the schedule by hand when the week, the weather, or how they feel gets in the way, including skipping a hard run. During the run, a coach's audio can play, and the phone can also give audio updates for pace, time, or distance. The log keeps duration, splits, a map or an indoor graph, and, on a recent run, effort, surface, shoe, and notes. Nike's New Zealand app page says the app is free to download with no in-app purchases. Nothing in the Nike sources describes the app rewriting the remaining days from a missed workout, a fatigue score, or the logged result, and nothing describes an injury history that changes the runs.

Strava's help center, last updated 17 September 2026, says that effective 10 July — the year is not stated — athletes will no longer be able to create new running training plans on the Strava website, and that those plans are powered by Runna. An athlete already on a Strava web running plan can finish it. Recording and sharing an activity is free. On the phone, audio is split times and, for subscribers, segment updates, not a coach talking the runner through the workout. A subscriber can log how the effort felt on a 1–10 scale and, on the website, see a Fatigue score. That score is a chart. The help center does not say it changes a running plan. An injury history that changes runs was not found in primary sources.

## Nike Run Club

### How a plan is created

Nike Help says the app has training plans written by NRC coaches. The plans explain the goal of each training week and the purpose of every run. Every run also offers an Audio Guided Run. To start one, the runner taps Plans, selects a plan, and taps Start Plan. A plan can only be started in the phone app. After that, an individual run can be started on the phone or on Apple Watch. The same article also says that if the plans are missing, the runner should update the app and check back because they are coming soon, and that additional plans can be downloaded from Nike's training-plans page. Both instructions are on the live article. ([Does the NRC App Have Training Plans?](https://www.nike.com/help/a/nrc-plan))

The plans named on that help article, for the distances this runner cares about:

- An eight-week 5K plan. Each week mixes speed, long, and recovery runs.
- An eight-week 10K plan. Speed, long, and recovery runs, plus optional Nike Training Club workouts.
- A 14-week half-marathon plan, with up to five runs each week: easy, long, and speed.

The same article also lists a four-week Get Started plan, mostly easy runs with an occasional faster run, for someone curious about running or coming back from a break, and a second four-week plan with three audio guided runs. A marathon plan is listed too. It is outside this runner's distances. ([Does the NRC App Have Training Plans?](https://www.nike.com/help/a/nrc-plan))

The public plan pages agree on the shape of the 5K and the 10K and disagree with the PDF and the help article on the half marathon.

The 5K page says the plan is an eight-week schedule, that Guided Runs are built to adapt to the runner's experience level and are meant to be flexible, and that the runner can jump in four or eight weeks from race day. Nike recommends at least four weeks, and enough fitness to complete the programmed workouts. The runner "is in control of what you put into the program." The published sample weeks are five sessions: recovery, speed, recovery, speed, long. The speed sessions are intervals at named paces (5K, 10K, mile, best), with recoveries between them. ([5K Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/5k-training-plan))

The 10K page uses the same eight-week, four-or-eight-weeks-out, at-least-four-weeks wording. Its sample weeks are also five sessions of recovery, speed, and long runs, including a Headspace recovery run. ([10K Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/10k-training-plan))

The half-marathon page says the plan is six to 14 weeks, and that each week the runner can choose three, four, or five Guided Runs, from recovery through speed and long. The sample weeks on that page list five sessions. ([Half-Marathon Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/half-marathon-training-plan))

The downloadable PDFs, which the plan pages point at, fix a weekly count of five runs:

- 5K PDF: eight weeks; jump in at four or eight weeks; at least four weeks; "each week you will have 5 runs"; two recovery runs and two rest days. ([5K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))
- 10K PDF: the same eight-week window and five runs a week; two recovery runs and two rest days. ([10K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-10K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))
- Half-marathon PDF: designed around 14 weeks; jump in at eight or 14 weeks from race day; Nike recommends at least six weeks; "each week you will have 5 runs"; two recovery runs and two rest days. ([Half-marathon PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-Half-Marathon-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))

So Nike's own half-marathon page says the runner chooses three, four, or five runs inside a six-to-14-week window, the PDF says five runs inside a 14-week plan the runner may enter late, and Nike Help says up to five runs in a 14-week plan. The sources were not reconciled further.

Pace targets are a chart the runner looks up, not a result the sources say the app computes from later logs. The PDFs say to pick a row from a recent 5K, 10K, half-marathon, or marathon time (the last month or two), from average pace on a few runs in the Nike Running app (that average becomes recovery-day pace), or from an educated guess. That row is "your starting point." Each target is the middle of a range. The same pages say paces can move with fitness, weather, elevation, stress, fatigue, and lack of sleep, and that the runner should listen to the body rather than the numbers on the watch or phone. They also say fitness and pace will, the writers hope, increase as the weeks go on. They do not say the app replaces the row after a logged run. ([5K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [10K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-10K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [Half-marathon PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-Half-Marathon-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))

The overview page describes the plans as living in the Nike Run Club app, and as including Guided Runs plus mindset, nutrition, and recovery tips. ([Running Training Plans](https://www.nike.com/running/training-plans))

### Whether the plan changes after a miss, fatigue, or a logged result

Nike says each of these plans was built to adapt to the runner's experience level and to be flexible. The pages explain that as entering partway through (four or eight weeks out on the 5K and 10K; eight or 14 on the half-marathon PDF) and as the runner modifying the schedule. Automatic rescheduling after a missed day, a fatigue input, or a logged result: **not found in primary sources**.

What the PDFs do say is that the runner changes the plan. Schedule, weather, and how the runner feels vary, "so modify this plan to your needs." Rest days are for taking the day off, doing a Nike Training Club routine, or running a few easy miles. "Sometimes you will need to adjust the program to fit what you need. And yes, that means that sometimes the best speed run or long run will be no run." The schedule is "written in pencil," and "you are also the Head Coach." Some days the runner does the recommended training, and some days more or less. "The best coach for you is you." Recovery runs are "easy and based on how you feel." ([5K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [10K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-10K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [Half-marathon PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-Half-Marathon-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))

On fatigue specifically, the PDFs say feeling fatigued is normal as training progresses, and that the response they describe is sleep, food, hydration, recovery days, and shoes. "Sometimes the best training is to focus on recovery and rest." Fatigue is also one of the reasons a pace target should not be treated as exact. ([5K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [10K PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-10K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf), [Half-marathon PDF](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-Half-Marathon-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf))

The 5K and 10K pages tell the runner that Guided Runs give "the guidance you need to listen to your body, adapt to your training plan, and become your own best coach." The adapting in that sentence is the runner's. ([5K Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/5k-training-plan), [10K Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/10k-training-plan))

A logged run can store an effort, described in the next section. No Nike page read for this note says that stored effort changes the remaining plan.

([Nike Run Club App, Nike New Zealand](https://www.nike.com/nz/nrc-app)) Headspace sessions also appear by name in the 10K and half-marathon sample weeks. ([10K Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/10k-training-plan), [Half-Marathon Training Plan](https://www.nike.com/running/half-marathon-training-plan))

A Nike editorial page last updated 7 June 2022 says guided runs include tips, stories, and inspiration on distance-based or time-based runs, that the runner can play their own music in the background or an NRC playlist, and that runs can be downloaded for poor service. That page is four years older than this note. Where a current product or help page covers the same point, the current page is the one cited above. ([How to Set and Achieve New Running Goals With the Nike Run Club App](https://www.nike.com/a/running-goals))

## Strava

### How a plan is created, and whether it changes

Strava's article "Training Plans for Runners" was last updated 17 September 2026 (the page payload's `lastUpdatedDate`; the visible label is "Updated over 3 weeks ago"). It says:

> Effective July 10th, athletes will no longer be able to create new running training plans on the Strava website. These plans will now be powered by Runna.

The article does not give a year for 10 July. It says athletes already on a Strava web running plan can finish that plan. When it ends, or if the athlete ends it early, a new plan has to be created in Runna. Support for the existing training-plans feature ends once those current plans are finished. Runna is not part of the standard Strava subscription. A Strava + Runna bundle is offered. The code STRAVA-TP is called a 2-week free trial code and is also described as unlocking an extra free week. Free athletes and subscribers can use it, unless they have already redeemed a Runna trial. The article says only new Runna users get that extended trial. Cycling plans are unchanged: a Carmichael Training Systems plan can still be created on the Strava website. ([Training Plans for Runners](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401942-training-plans-for-runners))

What those leftover Strava web running plans do after a missed day, fatigue, or a logged result: **not found in primary sources**. The miss-a-workout instructions on Strava's site are on the cyclists' article: rearrange days or skip days, but not too many, and past workouts stay in the inbox. That article is about cycling plans. ([Training Plans for Cyclists](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401867-training-plans-for-cyclists)) It is not evidence for the running plans.

A second Strava page, "What Features Are Included in a Strava Subscription?", dated 10 September 2026, still lists "Training Plans: For running and cycling to help you achieve your fitness goals." It does not mention the Runna move. ([subscription features](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402044-what-features-are-included-in-a-strava-subscription)) The runners article is the one that describes the current running-plan path, and it is the later of the two.

What Runna itself does with a missed workout is background, below. It is not a Strava plan.

### Guidance during a run, including audio

Recording is documented for the Strava mobile app. The runner opens Record, picks the sport, and taps Start. For a GPS activity the screen switches between map and stats. Live stats for a run show split average pace. A GPS halo shows signal accuracy. The bottom sheet includes Beacon (real-time location to up to three contacts), a Bluetooth heart-rate sensor, screen-on settings, audio cues, and auto-pause. Finish opens a save screen: title, photos, description, privacy, and more. Off-route alerts appear on the phone's record screen when a saved route is loaded and the runner leaves it by a meaningful distance. They are on by default. ([How do I Record an Activity on Strava?](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402137-how-do-i-record-an-activity-on-strava))

Live elevation on that record screen is listed for hiking, trail running, specified cycling types, and winter sports. Road running is not in that list.

Audio cues, enabled from Record, then Customize, then Audio Cues:

- Start, stop, and pause announcements for runs, rides, and walks.
- Half-mile or half-kilometer, or full mile or kilometer, announcements. This option is only for Run and Trail Run.
- Live Segments: real-time updates on starred and popular segments while recording. Strava marks Live Segments as a subscription feature.

([How Do I Enable Audio Announcements on Strava?](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402180-how-do-i-enable-audio-announcements-on-strava), dated 10 September 2026)

A coach narrating the workout, pace targets for an interval session, or audio that changes because the runner is tired: **not found in primary sources**. The subscription feature list's "Live Performance Data" is speed, distance, and location in real time on the phone. ([subscription features](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402044-what-features-are-included-in-a-strava-subscription))

While recording, the app uses GPS and does not use cellular data unless maps are viewed or Live Segments or Beacon are on. After the run, the activity syncs. Strava's example size is about 1 MB for two hours. ([recording article](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402137-how-do-i-record-an-activity-on-strava))

### What a run log contains

On save, the phone collects a title, photos, a description, and privacy. ([recording article](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402137-how-do-i-record-an-activity-on-strava))

The activity details page, on the website or the mobile app, "includes your activity's date and time, statistics, biometrics, analysis charts, map, segment matches, achievements, times, and elevation profile, among other metrics." A logged-out web viewer, when the profile is public, can see activity, distance, elevation, moving time, calories, photos, map, elevation profile, kudos, comments, and an achievement count. ([activity privacy controls](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401987-how-do-my-activity-privacy-controls-work-on-strava))

Moving time and elapsed time are both shown. Elapsed time includes stops. For a run, if the runner paused, Strava uses that paused timer time as moving time and does not strip further rest. If the runner never paused, Strava calculates moving time from GPS. Pace in the feed and on activity screens generally uses moving time. A run tagged as a race uses elapsed time. Segments and Best Efforts use elapsed time. While recording a run, the phone's accelerometer detects running motion. ([Moving Time, Speed, and Pace Calculations](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401804-moving-time-speed-and-pace-calculations))

Perceived Exertion is a 1–10 value from "Easy" to "Max Effort," entered on the save or edit screen. On the website it is a slider. On the phone the prompt is "How did that activity feel?" It can stand in for heart rate. The runner can add it to past activities one by one, not in bulk. ([Perceived Exertion](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401767-perceived-exertion))

Private notes are visible only to the athlete. Strava's privacy FAQ says to use them when the description cannot be private while the activity stays public. Hidden details, per activity or as a default, can include heart rate, pace or speed, power, calories, and start time. ([privacy FAQ](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401776-strava-s-privacy-controls-faq), [hide details](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401769-hide-details-from-your-activities))

A manual activity is a sport plus time and distance, on the website or the phone, for a run that was not recorded with GPS. On the website, start time, distance, duration, and elevation of a manual entry cannot be edited; the athlete deletes it and creates another, which drops kudos, comments, and photos. On the phone, start time, distance, and duration can be edited, and elevation cannot. ([Uploading a manual activity](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402188-how-do-i-upload-a-manual-activity-to-strava))

Subscriber analysis named on the subscription page, beyond the log itself: Relative Effort, Fitness and Freshness, the Training Log, matched activities, cumulative stats, grade-adjusted pace, custom heart-rate zones, workout analysis, pace analysis, and Best Efforts. Best Efforts are marked mobile-only. ([subscription features, 10 September 2026](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402044-what-features-are-included-in-a-strava-subscription))

Relative Effort is on the activity page for a subscriber when the activity has heart rate or a Perceived Exertion. It is a cardiovascular-work score, personalized to heart-rate zones, so a short hard run can compare with a long easy one. The weekly graph suggests a range from the three-week average. Below the range is described as taking it easy and as a down week for recovery. Above the range is straining more than usual, with a note to recover afterward. ([Relative Effort](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401794-relative-effort))

The Training Log, for subscribers, updates when an activity uploads. Activities are circles on a weekly view. Filters include sport, and whether the number shown is time, distance, or elevation. Tags include commutes, recovery activities, and activities with pets. Tags are added on the activity edit screen. ([Training Log](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402077-training-log))

### Injury history and fatigue

An injury history stored on the athlete, or used to change a run: **not found in primary sources**.

Two Strava pages mention injury, and neither is an injury history:

- The subscription feature list includes "Recover Athletics: Prevent injury with access to custom exercises from Recover Athletics." ([subscription features](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402044-what-features-are-included-in-a-strava-subscription))
- The Recover Athletics article says that app "helps thousands of athletes solve nagging pain and prevent injuries," and that its activities can upload to Strava from the connection date forward. Individual past activities cannot be synced. The article does not say Strava asks about past injuries or edits a plan from them. ([Recover Athletics and Strava](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401714-recover-athletics-and-strava))

Fatigue is a score, not a plan change. Fitness and Freshness, a subscriber chart on the website, tracks Fitness, Fatigue, and Form. Fatigue is described as "that tired feeling that limits your performance," modeled like fitness on a shorter timescale: it rises after a couple of hard days and falls after a few days off. Form is Fitness minus Fatigue. The chart is built from Training Load (power) and/or Relative Effort (heart rate or Perceived Exertion). A runner with no power data gets the chart from Relative Effort alone. Clicking a date shows the activities that contributed. Races are marked in red. The help center does not say this chart edits a training plan. ([How Fitness and Freshness is Calculated](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402032-fitness-freshness))

The phone's Fitness chart uses the same inputs and decays when the athlete takes time off. Strava says the Fitness score will not necessarily show overtraining, because it keeps rising as training rises, and points the athlete at the Relative Effort graph "to determine whether you're increasing your activity too quickly and at risk of injury or overtraining." ([Fitness](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401765-fitness))

Perceived Exertion is the athlete's own statement of how hard the activity felt. A recovery tag can be put on an activity in the Training Log. Neither source says the next run changes because of them.

The bundle is annual only. It is bought on Strava's website or in the Strava mobile app, not in Runna. A Family Plan does not include Runna. Canceling a separate Runna subscription in order to move onto the bundle does not, Strava says, affect the training plan. ([Subscription Preview](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401564-strava-subscription-preview))

Beacon on the Strava app is free. Beacon on compatible devices is listed with the subscription. ([subscription features](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402044-what-features-are-included-in-a-strava-subscription))

## Background

These products are not the comparison. They are here because a reader of the Nike and Strava facts will meet their names, and because Strava's own help center now points running plans at Runna.

### Runna

Strava's bundle FAQ says the bundle includes "personalized running plans built by expert coaches" and "real-time audio coaching during workouts," plus chat support in the Runna app. ([Strava and Runna Subscription FAQs](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401576-strava-and-runna-subscription-faqs))

On Runna's own site, the pricing page says the runner sets a distance or a race date and Runna builds a plan from experience and schedule. The plans "adapt to your performance." The primary price block on that page is $19.99 a month or $119.99 a year, each with a free 7-day trial. The page also lists other currencies. ([Runna pricing](https://www.runna.com/pricing))

Runna's help center, in an article updated 10 September 2026, says a single missed workout can be rearranged on the training calendar within about a week either way, or marked skipped. Skipping one workout, unless it is a key long run, "won't make too much difference," and Runna says not to adjust future workouts for that one skip. Missing more than three workouts or a full week, left unticked, can raise a Plan Realignment prompt, often on a Monday. The choices described include skipping the missed workouts and continuing, rearranging them, extending the plan, rebuilding it toward the same end date, or continuing unchanged. After more than about a month away, Runna recommends a new plan or a restart, because the plan keeps progressing in the app while the runner is away. Confirmed realignment cannot be undone. ([Plan Realignment](https://support.runna.com/en/articles/10026375-how-to-use-the-plan-realignment-feature))

### Garmin running plans

Garmin's blog, 5 June 2026, describes two products inside Garmin Connect, both used with a compatible Garmin watch.

Garmin Coach covers a 5K, a 10K, or a half marathon. The runner selects a race goal and a level from beginner through a 7-minute race pace. Workouts sync to the watch and "adjust based on your performance as your fitness improves." The named coaches are Jeff Galloway, Greg McMillan, and Amy Parkerson-Mitchell. Videos from the coach are part of the plan.

Garmin Run Coach asks for an end date or a goal and for the days to train or rest. Daily workouts "adapt to your performance, recovery and health metrics." Garmin says the watch should be worn as much as possible, including sleep, so the plan can use VO2 max, lactate threshold, training history, sleep, stress, and recovery. A poor night and poor recovery can become a lighter day. A rested, low-stress day can become a harder one. Optional strength workouts can be added.

([How to Use Garmin Coach Training Plans for Runners](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/blog/how-to-use-garmin-coach-training-plans-for-runners/))

### Apple running plans

A race-distance running plan that changes after missed days, fatigue, or logged pace: **not found in primary sources** on the Apple pages read for this note.

What Apple documents under Fitness+ is a Custom Plan: a schedule of Fitness+ workouts. Premade plans are Stay Consistent (from the subscriber's history and preferences), Push Further (longer versions of those activity types), and Get Started. A plan the subscriber builds asks for days, total time per day, length, and up to five activity types, plus trainer and music preferences. After it exists, a workout can be swapped on a day that already has activities. Workouts from earlier days or weeks can be completed during the plan. The plan can be ended, then repeated as the same workouts or refreshed with newer ones. ([Use Custom Plans in Apple Fitness+](https://support.apple.com/guide/fitness-plus/use-custom-plans-apdf222051d8/ios))

Separately, the iPhone Fitness app can build one custom run — warmup, work and recovery intervals, cooldown, and time, distance, or calorie goals — and a Pacer run that shows whether the runner is ahead of or behind a chosen pace. That is a single workout, not a multi-week race plan. ([Customize a workout in Fitness on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/customize-a-workout-iphbcd56be45/ios))
